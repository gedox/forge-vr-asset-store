# Forge VR Asset Store

A storefront for VR-ready 3D asset packs. Creators upload packs of `.glb` files — most of
them produced by the Forge VR Asset Generator desktop app, which publishes straight into
this store over its API — and browsers of the site pick through them by section, look at
the meshes in the browser, and download what they want.

## Design Context

### Users

Two people, with opposite jobs:

- **The creator** arrives with a pack already uploaded from Forge and wants to make it
  presentable: pick the six or seven assets that represent it, write a line about what it
  is, drop the ones that came out badly. This is inventory work — it should feel like
  arranging a shelf, not filling in a form.
- **The browser** is a game or VR developer scanning for something specific: "do they have
  crates", "is this pack actually 40 props or 40 variations of one". They judge in seconds,
  from thumbnails and numbers, and they want to spin the mesh before committing to a
  download. They will never read a paragraph of marketing copy.

Both are technical. Neither needs to be told what a triangle count is.

### Brand Personality

**A serious, professional storefront** in the register of a large platform company —
Meta, Unity, Activision. Calm, competent, high-trust. It states facts — 42 assets, 6.1 MB,
uploaded March 4 — and lets the objects do the selling. No superlatives, no marketplace
urgency, no exclamation marks. The voice is the voice of a platform that assumes you know
what you are buying.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Clean white corporate.** A bright white ground, near-black ink type, a single confident
blue accent, and hairline rules doing the work that boxes and shadows usually do. The
companion desktop app is a dark forge-lit tool; the store is the bright, polished product
that ships out of it. No dark mode — the store is deliberately, permanently light.

- **Type**: *Archivo* throughout — a modern grotesque used for both display and body.
  Display sizes are tight-tracked and bold; numbers are always tabular. Nothing is
  decorative; the type does the hierarchy.
- **Colour**: white (`#ffffff`), near-black ink (`#0b0f19`), cool greys for secondary text
  and hairlines, and one accent: brand blue (`#2563eb`) used for primary actions, active
  state and marks only, never as decoration.
- **Structure**: a sticky top bar with a wordmark and a small blue logo mark, a section
  index of pill tabs, and a responsive card grid of packs. Each pack is a white card with a
  preview mosaic, a title, a one-line description and a compact facts row; the newest pack
  is promoted to a full-width lead.
- **Texture**: none beyond white ground, hairlines and a soft shadow on hover. The 3D
  previews are the only visually rich objects on the page, and nothing competes with them.

**Anti-references**: the dark marketplace with neon accents and glassmorphic cards; the
warm "letterpress on paper" catalogue; identical rounded card grids with an icon above
every heading; hero sections that say "Discover amazing 3D assets".

### Design Principles

1. **The assets are the only images.** Every other surface is type and rules. If a
   decorative element appears that is not an asset preview, it is wrong.
2. **Facts over adjectives.** Counts, sizes, dates and formats, set in tabular figures.
   The description field is one line and the interface treats it as optional.
3. **Hairlines, not heavy boxes.** Structure comes from rules and spacing. Shadows are
   reserved for hover elevation and genuine depth.
4. **The accent marks state.** Active section, focus ring, selected cover asset, upload
   progress, the primary button. Nowhere else.
5. **Editing is direct.** A creator changes a pack where they see it — click the cover
   tile to promote it, click × on an asset to remove it. No modal edit screens.
6. **Motion decelerates.** Exponential ease-out, transform and opacity only, and it stops
   entirely under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
